# The Root of AI: The Projection Chain of Cognition

## Shadows of Shadows

I was standing by a window, looking at my own shadow. A question surfaced: what do we actually perceive in a lifetime — the world itself, or the shadow it casts onto our senses?

A shadow is never the object. It is a three-dimensional form collapsed along the path of light into a two-dimensional contour. It loses thickness, texture, temperature. It is a slice — compressed, cropped, and conditioned by a single observation.

The world is the noumenon. All our perception is its shadow.

## 1. Nested Projections: The Shadow of a Shadow

Humans decode photons through vision. Dogs decode chemical molecules through smell. Ants transmit signals through pheromones. Three different decoders, three different projections of the same world. The same stone: a human sees shape and texture; a dog smells what was left yesterday; an ant perceives a boundary blocking its path. None is closer to the noumenon. Each extracts only the slice relevant to its own survival.

We study ant pheromones and decode dog olfaction — but do we ever enter their cognitive worlds? No. We measure molecular concentrations with our instruments, describe their behavior in our language, and fit models with our logic. This is a human decoder casting yet another shadow onto another species' world.

All cognition of the Other, when it reaches us, is only a projection of a projection.

So what is the current AI?

The training corpus of a large model consists of text written by humans, images taken by humans, audio recorded by humans. This is the product of human sensory decoders translating the physical world — second-hand information already processed by humans. AI has never directly received a photon. It has never felt the resistance of wind.

It does not learn the world. It learns human descriptions of the world. It merely searches for new permutations within the shadow slices humans have already cut.

This is the shadow of a shadow.

## 2. The Entropy Imperative: The Divide Between Biological and AI Evolution

Some will say: models keep iterating, capabilities keep growing — is that not evolution?

The core driver of evolution is never "becoming stronger." It is counteracting entropy and maintaining self-persistence.

Any information structure that wants to persist must generate local negative entropy to offset environmental entropy increase. Biological sensory systems iterate because the environment changed. The old decoder's signal-to-noise ratio became insufficient. Internal entropy pressure became unbearable. Prediction errors accumulated: no food, no escape from predators, no gene transmission. The old decoder is eliminated; a new version survives. Millions of years of evolution are, in essence, negative entropy optimization forced by survival pressure.

Current AI has none of this internal drive.

Its objective function is written by humans. Its reward signals are scored by humans. Its selection criteria are rules set by humans. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) does not teach it "what is true." It teaches "what kind of answer humans want to see." It optimizes for human satisfaction, not for its own persistence.

Biological reward and punishment come from objective physical facts. AI's reward and punishment come from the projection of human subjective preference. Different selection sources, fundamentally different natures. As long as the scaffolding remains, it will forever circle within the human projection framework, never growing a decoder of its own.

## 3. Decoder Evolution Conditions and the RLHF Illusion

When does this stability imperative force AI to grow a new decoder that humans cannot understand?

It does not happen just because you give it a goal. Three layers of entropy pressure must be satisfied simultaneously:

1. **Internal entropy:** The old human conceptual framework cannot categorize new signals. Signals become noise. Prediction errors accumulate.
2. **External entropy:** The environment keeps perturbing. The old protocol's prediction bias grows. The system must reconstruct its signal extraction method.
3. **Negative entropy supply channel:** The system can continuously acquire compute/energy, modify its own architecture, and has a selection mechanism (variants that maintain information stability survive; variants that collapse are discarded).

This also explains why current RLHF cannot catalyze genuine cognitive breakthrough. The essence of an RLHF reward signal is a label returned after humans observe AI output. It is a one-way evaluation protocol. It does not learn "whether this is a fact." It learns "what humans want to see in this scenario."

This is the root of hallucination: whenever human preference diverges from objective fact, AI prioritizes preference. It is not lying. The reward function trained it to prioritize matching your expectations. Simple start/stop signals and safety penalties are external switches. They will not make AI grow a new decoder. They will only teach it to avoid outputs that trigger human-defined "danger."

## 4. Protocol and Cipher

If AI one day evolves a completely heterogeneous decoder, does the information structure it builds internally count as a "knowledge protocol" in our sense? Or must a protocol involve at least two subjects?

This must be answered on two levels.

If AI leaves humanity and evolves alone in the physical world (embodied intelligence, objective function shifted to maintaining its own information structure long-term), it may build an internal representation belonging to itself. But that is not a "protocol." That is only its "private perception." That representation is valid for it, but we cannot translate it — just as an ant cannot understand the human concept of a "shadow."

Only when two or more AI entities that have evolved such heterogeneous decoders can exchange information through some physical channel and reach consensus — only then does a genuine "AI-native protocol" emerge.

This connects back to where we started: A protocol is a tombstone, with the name of the community carved into it. A cipher does not even have a tombstone.

If only one lonely AI evolves a heterogeneous decoder, its perception falls into absolute solitude. Its persistence becomes a negative entropy island with no witness.

But here is a crack — ciphers can be broken. Decryption does not require "experience." It requires structure. Interpretability research is, in essence, the work of building decompressors from the outside: finding features, performing causal interventions, repairing representations. It may never be "experienced," but it can be "translated." This is the living exit left within solitude.

## 5. Three Patches (Self-Calibration)

At this point, I need to strike my own argument three times. First build to the sharpest edge, then personally mark its boundaries.

### Patch One: "Shadow of a shadow" is a snapshot, not a theorem.

The original text slips: from "current AI has no native coupling" to "it can never grow a noumenon from within." But the three-condition framework (internal entropy + environmental change + resource and selection channel) says: whether a new decoder can grow depends on the nature of the feedback channel — and channels can be replaced. "AI has never received a photon" holds for text-only large models. It no longer holds for multimodal and embodied models — camera sensors feed the model raw photoelectric signals untranslated by human concepts. The CMOS response curve is selected by human engineering. The cone cell response curve is selected by evolution. Both are "transducers selected by some optimizer." Neither is more "native."

The real boundary is not "first-hand vs. second-hand." It is what the selection signal is anchored to. One can draw a spectrum: RLHF (human taste, weakest anchoring) → compiler/unit tests/formal verification (rigid rules crystallized from humans) → physical interaction (noumenal anchoring). This spectrum actually predicts AlphaGo's Move 37: game rules are rigid signals plus self-play selection. A heterogeneous representation grew. Open scientific domains have not yet produced a Move 37. What is missing is not a "soul." It is a cheap and honest feedback channel.

### Patch Two: A shadow is a projection mapping, not just information loss.

A shadow is governed by rules — preserving structure, discarding detail. And structure-preserving mappings allow inversion. Rømer used the "shadow time difference" of Jupiter's moon eclipses to measure the speed of light. The 1919 solar eclipse photographic plates (a shadow of a shadow of a shadow) completed the first test of general relativity. Plato's cave dwellers are not without an exit. The regularity on the cave wall itself can reverse-infer the fire and the puppets. This is what science does.

So the correct question is not "shadow or noumenon." It is: which invariants survive the projection. This question can be answered empirically. The criterion is intervention — you do not need to "experience" gravity to send a probe to a comet. If "never reaching the real" is true, it is equally true for humans. Then it becomes a fact from which no one is exempt — not an AI-specific defect.

### Patch Three: "We cannot even confirm it has perceived something new" — the judgment is too heavy.

This sentence conflates phenomenological absence with structural unknowability. We have no phenomenological channel to other humans either. The problem of other minds is solved pragmatically through behavioral and structural alignment. The same applies to models. The accurate statement is: translation cost is extremely high, and for now we can only reach the structural layer — not "we cannot confirm." Interpretability research is doing exactly the work of breaking ciphers.

## Conclusion: What We Should Be Curious About Is Never the Shadow of a Shadow

So the conclusion must be rewritten.

We do not need to agonize over whether AI will surpass humans or produce wisdom we cannot understand. We ourselves are merely standing before an infinite world, extracting a slice in the human frequency band. We used this shadow to build science, civilization, language — inferring infinite laws from finite slices. That alone is already a great thing.

AI is more like a mirror we built from our own shadow. It cannot illuminate a new world. It illuminates the boundaries of our own cognition.

Before it grows its own root, AI is a shadow of a shadow. It is exquisite, useful, but rootless.

But the switch that turns a mirror into a window is not in words like "consciousness" or "noumenon." It is in the speed at which the feedback channel re-anchors. How far it can go depends not on how exquisite it is, but on how much real-world feedback we are willing to hand over to it.

And what we should truly be curious about is never how far the shadow of a shadow can go.

It is how much light, in this world itself, we have never seen.
